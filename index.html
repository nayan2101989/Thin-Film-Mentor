Builder’s recipe: blade-coated MAPbI₃ photodetector

From the builder’s M.A.Sc. research at Dalhousie. The mentor uses these conditions as its first reference for MAPbI₃ on glass and ITO.

Process sequence

  1. Clean glass or patterned ITO: sonicate in DI water with Triton detergent, then DI water, acetone, IPA and DI water again (15–20 min each). Dry with N₂. UV-ozone for 20 min.
  2. Make the solution: 0.3 M MAPbI₃ in DMSO (per mL: about 47.7 mg MAI and 138 mg PbI₂). Stir at 70 °C for at least 12 h.
  3. Blade coat in air: fume hood, RH below 20%. Put 60 µL between an angled Si blade and the heated substrate. Substrate 150–160 °C, blade speed 0.125–0.15 mm/s, gap tuned within 20–500 µm.
  4. Anneal: move to an N₂ glovebox, 100 °C for 20 min.
  5. Optional contact layer: spiro-OMeTAD (80 mg/mL in chlorobenzene with LiTFSI) blade coated at 0.075 mm/s, no heat. Then about 50 nm Au evaporated at 10⁻⁶ torr, 0.5–1 Å/s.

What the conditions did

  1. Grain size grew with substrate temperature (140→170 °C) and shrank with faster blade speed. Grains reached the mm scale, aligned with the shearing direction.
  2. At 180 °C and slow speed the film burned at grain boundaries and XRD showed PbI₂ peaks. Stay below the DMSO boiling point (about 189 °C).
  3. Slower speed (0.125 mm/s) gave thicker films (about 790 nm) and better responsivity and stability than 0.15 mm/s (about 533 nm).
  4. Blade-coated films oriented along (112)/(200), parallel to the substrate. Spin-coated reference films showed (110)/(220) and grains under 20 µm.

Published work by the builder

  1. M. M. Hasan, C. Clegg et al., G. I. Koleilat, “Stable Efficient Methylammonium Lead Iodide Thin Film Photodetectors with Highly Oriented Millimeter-Sized Crystal Grains,” ACS Photonics 7, 57–67 (2020). DOI: 10.1021/acsphotonics.9b01455. Blade-coated mm-size MAPbI₃ grains: over 10× the responsivity of spin-coated films at high light intensity, and over 80% performance kept after 300 h in air with no additives.
  2. U. Kwon, M. M. Hasan et al., H. J. Park, “Investigation into the Advantages of Pure Perovskite Film without PbI₂ for High Performance Solar Cell,” Scientific Reports 6, 35994 (2016). DOI: 10.1038/srep35994 (open access). A printing-based two-step process gave pure perovskite with no leftover PbI₂, raising solar cell efficiency from 13.7% to 16.0%.
  3. M. M. Hasan, “High Performance Organometal Halide Perovskite Solar Cell Prepared by Controlling Solvent Evaporation,” M.Sc. thesis, Ajou University, Korea (2015). PDMS-stamp printing of MAI with controlled solvent evaporation: 13.7% forward-scan efficiency in the best cell, ahead of the spin-coated reference cells.
  4. M. M. Hasan, A. Hamja, M. K. Islam, “Soft Lithography Based Printed Methyl Ammonium Iodide Hybrid Organo Metal Perovskite Solar Cell without Hole Transporting Layer,” ICMERE 2015, Chittagong, Bangladesh (2015). PDMS-stamp printing of MAI on PbI₂ gave 7.9% efficiency with no hole transport layer; the 100 °C conversion temperature was the key setting.
